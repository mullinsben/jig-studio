A kibbutz of capybaras · Olympic Mountains · members only

We live in a hot spring. You may visit.

We are thirty-one capybaras in an old-growth valley in the Olympic Mountains. The water is warm, the trees are older than your country, and there is no road. Membership is by invitation; the wait is six months. Yes, we are real. We do not know why you keep asking.

Meet the herd

Most of us were born here. We soak when the water is right, which is around four; we do not use clocks, we use Clementine. Thirty-one members, four pictured; the rest declined.

Nobody founded this place. The spring was here, the trees were here, and in 1987 the first of us walked in from somewhere and sat down. The Forest Service has a file. They have chosen not to open it, and we respect that about them.

The water is 104 degrees and has never been anything else. The yuzu grow on the far slope and are tended by David’s cousin, whom nobody has met. The oranges arrive on Thursdays. We do not ask from where; the committee has advised us that this is a committee matter.

Membership is simple. You ask. In about six months, if the duck is in a mood, you are told where the trailhead is that week. You come. You sit in the water with us until you understand. Most people take an afternoon. Gary took four days and is now a member in a different sense.

Become one of us

This is a kibbutz. Members share the water, the oranges, the work, and the floor; the yuzu is not shared, see rule four. Dues become oranges, hoof care, and yuzu from David’s cousin, who is also David. Pay early and we move you up the list. We are aware this is a bribe; we are rodents, not saints.

The annex

When the water gets to be too much

Nobody can soak forever; we have tried. Up the path there is a dry room full of puppies and kittens who do not swim and do not plan to. You sit on the floor. They decide the rest.

The stack

Three kittens, one decision. They move as a unit. The bottom one is in charge.

Apply for membership

Apply, and in about six months David will write back; he signs as a capybara. Directions come as a map drawn by the one who can hold a pencil; the trailhead moves because David moves it. Bring a towel. Leave the phone. Do not bring David a friend. He has asked.
